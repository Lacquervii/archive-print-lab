# Graphic chassis — fixed family grammar, not fake subject evidence

## Why this layer exists

A visual family can have a recognizable **graphic chassis**: a repeatable way that typography, flat planes, sparse marks and printed texture organize an image. That chassis is neither source content nor proof that the subject literally contains a frame, ruler, star or bracket.

Do not collapse these questions:

```text
What is the subject made of?            -> theme grammar
What does this carrier need to communicate? -> carrier grammar
How does this visual family organize a page? -> graphic chassis
```

The old modular prompt evidence for this family uses a hazy material image layer plus a crisp two-dimensional editorial overlay. Removing the overlay every time because it is not a literal branch, fold or seam erases a real part of the family. Reusing its exact purple rectangles and symbols every time is the opposite failure.

## Three valid provenance sources

A visible structural mark or field can be:

1. **Theme-native** — caused by the subject: branch directions, page planes, folds, contours, seams, shadows, paths, material boundaries.
2. **Carrier-native** — caused by the communication artifact: archive indexing, exhibition registration, catalog hierarchy, title plane, measured comparison, edition mark.
3. **Profile-native** — caused by the selected visual family's grammar: an oversized display plane, a small unequal set of translucent 2D planes, restrained dark blocks, short index words, hairline accents, paper/registration behavior.

A strong poster can combine all three. The requirement is not literal subject causality for every mark; it is **resolved visual causality**: each mark must belong to a declared theme, carrier or selected profile and must improve hierarchy, contact, reading direction or release.

## Diffused editorial chassis profiles

Select one intensity after routing the mode. These are defaults for this family, not universal requirements.

| Intensity | Use when | Visible behavior |
|---|---|---|
| `none` | narrative, quiet, or materially complete work needs no overlay | image/material hierarchy only; texture may remain |
| `light` | hero or material work needs a family trace but not a structural overlay | title or one plane, a few low-weight marks, printed finish |
| `active` | editorial cover needs a visible soft-image / crisp-graphic tension | typographic plane plus a small unequal field of translucent 2D planes, blocks or paths that contact the subject |
| `structural` | a 9010-like designed relational cover needs the chassis to carry real page pressure | authoritative macro type/glyph plane, visibly layered flat field, selected dark anchors, short index marks, authored release; keep geometry image-native and approximate across generators |

`active` and `structural` may use frames, rectangles, brackets, rules or small symbols **only as a resolved family vocabulary**. The canonical series uses `structural` as its default pressure level; explicit alternate modes may reduce it after recording why. These are flat printed planes, never a default HUD, product specification sheet, solid 3D cube system or edge-filling decoration.

## Translation rule

When a chassis profile is active, translate it through the current theme instead of copying source inventory:

| Keep | Recompile per theme |
|---|---|
| title as visual plane | title word, title fit, type character, contact point |
| unequal flat field | plane geometry, direction, opacity, relation to subject contours/shadows |
| localized printed contrast | accent color role, dark-anchor locations, material/ink relationship |
| sparse micro index | vocabulary, placement, whether it is needed at all |
| tactile registration/print finish | paper, ink, scan, textile, photographic or material host |

For example, a tree can receive a graphite-and-sage translucent plane field aligned to canopy lobes and root-shadow axes; it does not need literal silk-wrapping frames or purple stars. A computer can use broad screen/reflection-aligned planes while retaining one complete device; it does not become an exploded product sheet.

## Integration gates

A selected chassis passes only when all relevant statements are true:

- its intensity was selected from the carrier, mode and viewer job, not inherited blindly;
- at least one chassis member has a readable relation to the macro, central event or release;
- it has unequal scale, opacity, completion or depth rather than repeated equal furniture;
- it stays subordinate to the primary theme gestalt unless the chosen title plane is intentionally first;
- its palette, geometry and vocabulary have been translated to the current theme;
- removing all subject content would still leave a coherent page hierarchy, but removing the chassis would reduce the selected family character;
- it does not pretend to be factual information.

Reject it when it is merely a pile of aligned rectangles, fake labels, technical costume, universal purple, or copied source geometry.

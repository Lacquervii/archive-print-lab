# Quality rubric

## Score independently

### Mode fit

- Does the selected mode match the viewer action and subject behavior?
- Is the density distribution appropriate to the mode rather than inherited from a recent reference?
- Does the chosen chassis intensity match the carrier and mode: absent when it would be costume, light when it should only preserve family continuity, active/structural when the selected cover needs a real soft-image / crisp-printed tension?
- Is typography doing the selected job: structural, explanatory, indexical, optional, or absent?
- Would another mode make the subject more truthful or readable? If yes, diagnose a router or recipe failure before editing details.

### Rendering-style branch fit

Judge the selected surface atmosphere independently from the series skeleton:

- the branch was resolved before generation, with its selection source recorded;
- the field temperature, diffusion, print behavior and texture host match the selected branch;
- `contemporary_frosted` does not leak into warm antique paper or nostalgic distress, and `warm_analog_print` does not leak into unlicensed cool clinical sterility;
- branch treatment changes surface atmosphere only; it must not erase the macro → meso → subject → release hierarchy, central pressure, soft/hard separation or fresh theme palette;
- a branch match cannot compensate for a failed canonical structure, and a structurally valid poster with an unselected branch is a revise/fail result.


- Are transferable relationships present?
- Did source-specific content, palette, title, or geometry leak?
- Does the new work feel authored rather than reskinned?

### Carrier mechanics

- Is first attention clear?
- Does title/image/diagram/scene hierarchy fit the carrier?
- Is the title fit appropriate to its length, aspect ratio, subject width and reading distance: contained, edge-touching, cropped, stacked, secondary or absent?
- For poster or cover routes, does the carrier blueprint survive when the subject nouns are mentally hidden?
- Are macro, meso, subject, and micro layers distinguishable, with unequal visual weight and readable contact?
- Does density have a deliberate center and release?
- Is internal subject richness achieved inside a controlled outer footprint, with continuous usable title/margin/release corridors rather than accidental edge-to-edge occupation?
- Do structural marks and selected chassis members contact the subject, macro plane, information or release through a visible spatial job?
- Does the aspect ratio match the selected ratio and its declared source (`default_16_9` or `explicit_user_override`), rather than a backend convenience return?
- Does the selected ratio change the design intelligently rather than simply crop it?

- **Resolved-causality before decorative:** structural marks may be theme-native, carrier-native or profile-native, but each needs an identifiable declared job in the current page. A line, frame, label, tick, symbol or translucent plane that merely fills a gap, copies source furniture, pretends to be factual data, or announces “editorial” is a hard failure.
- **Chassis translation:** when the selected family chassis is active, preserve its page pressure by translating its type behavior, flat field, dark anchors, micro marks and print finish through the new subject. Do not delete all overlay pressure merely because the source's exact frames/stars/purple cannot transfer; do not preserve it by mechanically repeating those source motifs either.
- **Line-budget by need, not quota:** no minimum number of lines, frames, labels, or symbols exists. Use only the fewest carriers needed to establish hierarchy, contact, or reading direction; a quiet subject may require none.
- **Theme-specific field:** prioritize the subject’s own boundaries, planes, folds, contours, shadows, seams, paths, or material transitions. If a selected profile-native field is needed, it must complement rather than replace these forms; if the subject’s authentic field is soft or tonal, do not force it into crisp geometry.
- **Energy substitution:** when source-specific lines or frames are removed, the central theme and/or translated chassis must gain equivalent authored pressure through meaningful whole-form echoes, unequal scale, overlap, crop, material density, localized dark anchors, a resolved flat field or a subject-caused field. “Fewer marks” must not result in “less design.”
- **Canonical series fit:** when no explicit **mode** override exists, reject a result missing the upper title plane, compact central event, contacting meso field, soft/hard separation or authored release. A ratio-only override preserves these roles; record its selected ratio separately. If a mode override exists, record its reason and judge against that mode without claiming canonical-cover equivalence.
- **Whole-theme priority:** when an object or artifact is the viewer’s main subject, preserve its complete gestalt. Do not let component details or graphic scaffolds become the visual protagonist unless process, comparison, archive, or construction is the explicit task.
- **Bounded abundance:** a protected footprint must still contain a primary gestalt, a readable medium-scale relationship field made from theme, carrier and/or selected chassis members, and micro material/trace evidence. Reject a result that preserves margins merely by reducing the subject to a generic isolated icon.
- **Meso visibility:** the resolved meso field must be readable at normal viewing distance as an unequal compositional event. Do not count bark grain, lichen freckles, paper noise or tiny marks as a substitute for medium-scale form, shadow, fold, junction, plane, cavity, transition or a deliberately selected contacting chassis plane.

### Theme fit

- Is the current subject recognizable?
- Are material, anatomy, construction, motion, function, or cultural traits credible?
- Do related forms belong together?

### Layer and material fit

- Are soft/material and crisp/structural layers distinct when intended?
- Are contrast events localized and meaningful?
- Does texture have a host and a cause?

### Production fit

- Is displayed text short enough for the target generator’s approximate lettering, and is it reported honestly?
- Is the generator choice appropriate and, if none was named, was the prompt kept generic?
- Does the lower/right release carry a connected low-weight continuation and, on a canonical generated cover, the small `archive-print-lab` colophon without becoming a watermark?
- Does the Image2 adapter preserve the broad title plane, rich lateral center, unequal primary/support/return carriers and localized dark pressure?

- **Canonical series gate:** if the request did not explicitly override the mode, the output must preserve the same upper title → compact central event → contacting meso field → authored release skeleton. A visually attractive hero-like image is a failure of the series contract, not a pass.

Reject or revise when any relevant item is true:

- the selected mode contradicts the viewer action or subject behavior;
- a concentrated-cover scaffold, title plane, or cluster has been imposed on a hero, archive, material, process, narrative, or information task without cause;
- generic still life, bouquet, lineup, moodboard, or scene replaces the requested design system;
- title is too weak, too heavy, or detached from the carrier;
- the title fit is inappropriate for its length, aspect ratio, subject width, or reading distance, including an illegible full-bleed crop used without a compositional cause;
- in a concentrated cover, the first-attention plane is caption-like, the central family is scattered, or the structural carrier is only micro-decoration;
- central subject/information family lacks hierarchy, depth, crop, or contact;
- internal density has been achieved by expanding the subject footprint until title, margin, or release corridors collapse into accidental leftovers;
- graphic marks or chassis members are random decoration, technical costume, fake factual annotation, copied source geometry, or unresolved profile furniture;
- a selected active/structural chassis has been stripped into a weak title plus isolated polite subject, or inflated until it overwhelms the primary theme;
- lower or quiet region is accidental dead space or unmotivated clutter;
- an output’s selected ratio was not recorded, or its returned size was hidden or silently changed by a crop;
- global blur/black/sepia erases the subject;
- the selected rendering-style branch is missing, or an unselected atmosphere leaks into the result (for example retro warmth, sepia or heavy analog distress under `contemporary_frosted`);
- exact text is visibly wrong but reported as correct;
- vertical work becomes a column or top-to-bottom story when the reference calls for a broad event;
- vertical poster work leaves a long accidental tail below a narrow middle event;
- the result is attractive but below the reference’s completion level.

## Review order

```text
mode fit
→ viewer action
→ thumbnail first attention
→ carrier mechanics
→ subject hierarchy
→ material credibility
→ layer separation
→ palette
→ typography accuracy
→ finish
→ originality
```

## Iteration rule

Change only the top one or two failures per round. Name the failing block: engine, recipe, theme mechanics, theme grammar, composition, carrier, material, palette, or backend. Do not add generic adjectives to solve a structural problem.

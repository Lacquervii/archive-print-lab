# Poster mechanics — carrier before theme

## Scope

Load this module for `poster`, `cover`, and reference-derived editorial key-visual work, especially the **concentrated editorial cover** route. It is a carrier system, not a universal composition and not a subject template.

The reference may teach pressure, hierarchy, crop, contact, and release. The current subject still decides its own forms, material, palette, semantic marks, and title.

**Resolved field gate:** the structural field is a role, not a mandatory literal subject diagram. First name the current theme's authentic carriers—whole silhouette, page plane, fold, contour, shadow boundary, growth path, seam, threshold, alignment or material transition. Then decide whether the selected visual-family **graphic chassis** also needs a carrier-native/profile-native flat printed field. A selected chassis may use translucent planes, outline frames, brackets, sparse marks or registration behavior without pretending those forms literally grew from the subject. Its geometry, palette, density and contact must be re-authored through the new theme and communication carrier. Never add generic editorial furniture just to satisfy a scaffold requirement, but never erase needed family-level overlay pressure merely because a literal subject carrier is sparse.

**Energy substitution gate:** when source-specific furniture is removed, replace its pressure with a resolved mix of theme-native richness, carrier-native logic and/or selected chassis grammar—related whole forms, meaningful echoes, scale contrast, overlap, crop, material density, dark anchors, a translated flat field or a subject-specific field. Do not equate theme customization with sparse minimalism.

**Subject before scaffold:** on object and artifact routes, the complete subject must read as one authored gestalt before any component detail or external graphic marks are introduced. Component-like fragments are permitted only when the viewer action is explicitly process, comparison, archive, or construction.

## Core contract

A finished concentrated cover is one authored visual event, not a recognizable subject placed on a pale background. Build four interacting roles:

```text
first-attention plane
+ central event family
+ connected structural field
+ authored release
```

These are roles, not literal objects. The first-attention plane may be a title, a glyph mass, a dominant image event, or a material phenomenon. The structural field may be a family of contour systems, page planes, paths, joins, alignments, measurements, shadow geometries, **or a selected profile-native crisp printed chassis** translated through the current theme. Use `graphic-chassis.md` to keep this distinction explicit.

## Carrier blueprint before prompt writing

Write this internal blueprint before describing subject detail:

1. **First attention** — what is seen first at thumbnail size, how large it is, and what it interrupts or anchors.
2. **Central event family** — one dominant form or event with unequal supporting forms; front, middle, and rear relationships; meaningful overlap, crop, or contact.
3. **Structural field and chassis** — first declare whether each member is theme-native, carrier-native or profile-native. Build one coherent medium-scale field made of unequal planes, paths, blocks, frames or alignments only when the selected recipe/chassis needs it. It enters the central field and passes behind, under, through, aligns with, or is cut by the subject. Small labels and marks are subordinate to it. A profile-native member is legitimate only when it has a page job and has been re-authored for the theme; it is never fake factual data.
4. **Release** — a quieter lower or peripheral region with a deliberate job: breathing room, continuation, residue, transition, or reading pause. It is not the empty remainder after the model runs out of content.
5. **Weight map** — where visual pressure is concentrated and where the field is allowed to breathe. Use localized anchors and contrast events; do not darken or decorate everything to simulate intensity.

If the blueprint cannot be described without listing the subject nouns, the prompt is content-led. Rewrite the carrier first.

## The concentrated-cover rhythm

Use this as a default pressure profile only when the carrier and reference support it:

```text
upper structural event
→ compact middle event family
→ quieter but authored release
```

### Upper structural event

A title or glyph mass should behave as part of the composition, not as a caption floating above it. Its relationship to the frame is adaptive rather than predetermined: it may be contained, touch an edge, be partially cropped, bleed beyond the frame, stack across lines, become secondary, or be omitted. Choose the fit that preserves the title’s role, legibility and relationship with the current subject. A subject may interrupt a title, but the interruption should be intentional and the word should remain recoverable. Do not force a long word or narrow-format title into a full-bleed crop merely to imitate a reference.

### Structural carriers and selected chassis

A concentrated-cover structural field is not automatically a collection of hairlines, outlined squares, brackets, ticks, stars, labels, or registration marks. Those are optional surface carriers, not the definition of the field. But a soft material image layer against a crisp two-dimensional editorial overlay **is a documented family-level grammar** in this visual lineage; when the router selects `active` or `structural` chassis intensity, treating every flat overlay as forbidden generic furniture is an over-correction.

First name the current theme’s authentic carriers:

- a book may generate cover planes, page-block edges, folds, paper extensions, shadow boundaries, folio rhythm, or a reading axis;
- a computer may generate a whole silhouette, screen glow, reflection, shadow, or broad interface of planes only when those planes remain subordinate to the complete object;
- an organic subject may generate contour, growth path, overlap, anatomy, or habitat traces;
- architecture may generate thresholds, cavities, alignments, circulation, and shadow geometry;
- a material subject may generate grain, fold, fracture, pressure, translucency, or phase boundaries.

Then, if chassis intensity requires it, add only the smallest translated profile-native field that restores family pressure: a typographic plane, selected translucent printed planes, an unequal dark block, a few sparse index marks or registration behavior. Align its direction, opacity, palette, crop and contact to the current subject; do not reuse the source’s exact purple frames, stars, ticks, vocabulary or geometry. It may remain sparse, implicit or absent when the selected mode does not need it.

Use the smallest combined set of theme/carrier/chassis members that makes the poster more legible and more authored. If a line or frame has no declared page job, remove it. Do not use generic editorial marks to simulate completion.

### Compact middle event family

The subject should read as a designed family rather than a row, bouquet, product lineup, isolated hero object, or loose scene. Use one dominant form, unequal supports, scale difference, depth, local darkness or color pressure, and deliberate edge entry/crop. Richness belongs inside the meaningful family before it is added to the perimeter.

### Structural field in contact

Build one coherent medium-scale resolved field before micro marks. It may contain theme-native paths/planes, carrier-native communication geometry and selected profile-native chassis members: a primary plane or path, supporting frames or blocks, and lighter echoes. The field must be visible without zooming in and must interact with the family through shared alignment, interruption, occlusion, passage, or crop. Do not replace it with one isolated box, two faint hairlines, random frames, corner stickers, generic technical symbols, or source-shaped furniture.

### Authored release

Keep the release quieter than the central event, but let it carry a low-weight continuation: a faint alignment, small factual inscription, material residue, crop cue, or controlled texture. A release can be nearly empty when that emptiness is clearly shaped and justified; accidental dead space is not restraint.

## Two-density and footprint rule

Separate density inside the subject, the subject’s outer footprint, and density around it:

- **Internal subject density:** rich and specific — related forms, depth, overlap, material evidence, selected dark anchors, and focal detail.
- **Subject footprint:** bounded and deliberate — the outer silhouette or event envelope must leave continuous space for the title, margins and release. A dense center is not a license to expand the subject across the entire canvas.
- **External graphic/chassis density:** controlled and subordinate — one coherent resolved structural field with unequal members plus only the marks needed to complete the reading. Its members may be theme-native, carrier-native or selected profile-native; the provenance must be intentional.

Do not solve a weak center by adding perimeter furniture **or by enlarging the subject until the field is full**. Do not protect whitespace by deleting the subject family until it becomes a single icon. Build a complete internal system and the selected family-level graphic pressure inside a controlled envelope, then preserve only the quiet space that has a compositional job.

## Route by subject behavior

### Mechanics-first route

Use for books, artifacts, products, architecture, designed systems, and scenes that naturally tempt the model into a still life or a vertical story. Establish the field, first-attention structure, compact middle family, structural field, and release before filling in material nouns and object details.

The final model prompt should still be natural spatial prose. “Mechanics-first” is an internal construction order, not a request to expose a checklist or to make the image look like a diagram.

### Subject-first but carrier-locked route

Use for organic forms, bodies, gestures, materials, and events whose identity must lead. Let the subject grammar establish the main event, but keep the title/first-attention role, central family, contact layer, and release active. Subject-first does not mean subject-only.

## Vertical translation

A vertical canvas changes the available field; it does not automatically create a top-to-bottom narrative axis.

When the reference depends on a broad middle event, translate it as:

```text
wide upper structural event
+ broad lateral middle belt with side entry/crop
+ visible contact layer crossing the belt
+ short, intentional lower release
```

Keep the middle wide enough to read as a family. Do not let a stem, spine, tower, body axis, ribbon, or page axis consume the whole composition unless the brief explicitly requires that axis. A genuinely axial subject may retain its axis, but needs a lateral counterforce, overlap, or cross-structure so the page does not become a column.

## Prompt translation

After the internal blueprint is stable, write one continuous spatial description. Prefer visible verbs:

```text
spans, interrupts, enters from the edge, overlaps, passes behind, reappears,
aligns with, is cut by, releases into, recedes toward
```

A reliable natural sequence is:

```text
continuous carrier and field
→ current subject family and material evidence
→ central overlap, crop, and depth
→ title or first-attention structure
→ resolved theme-/carrier-/chassis graphic field
→ quiet release
→ brief-derived palette and finish
```

The sequence can change when the subject demands it, but it must describe one coherent space rather than an audit list. Keep internal pressure names, quotas, percentages, object counts, and quality scores out of model-facing prose. Use a short refusal block only for concrete failure modes that the chosen backend responds to.

## Production threshold

Use the completed image-native result of whatever generator the user chose. Keep title wording short and accept that model-generated lettering and line systems are approximate; never claim they are exact or deterministic.

## Review gates

Review in this order:

1. **Thumbnail:** first-attention plane, central mass, selected chassis pressure when relevant, and release are immediately legible.
2. **Normal size:** the subject family has hierarchy; any selected structural/chassis field is visible and in contact; the field is not globally filled, globally empty, copied source furniture or fake information.
3. **Close range:** material or subject evidence is credible; overlaps, crops and inscriptions are intentional; no layer feels pasted on.
4. **Comparison:** the result transfers the reference’s relationships without importing its nouns, title, palette, symbols, or exact geometry.

Classify as revise or fail for this route when the title is only a label, the middle is a generic still life or scattered lineup, the structural field disappears or floats, the vertical page becomes a narrow column, or the release is accidental dead space.

# Theme grammar router

Use this file to make the new subject specific without making the Skill subject-bound.

## Universal questions

For every theme define:

- what is recognizable at a glance;
- the dominant form or event;
- related forms that belong to one family;
- what must stay sharp;
- what may soften, repeat, crop, or echo;
- how depth and overlap naturally occur;
- which material details prove credibility;
- which graphic carriers have a real semantic cause;
- what belongs inside the subject’s visual envelope versus what must remain outside it;
- which continuous negative-space corridors must remain open for title, margin, release, or reading path;
- which common visual cliché must be blocked.

## Category routes

### Organic / living

Use silhouette, anatomy, growth, gesture, stages, habitat, or behavioral traces. Related forms may be leaves, limbs, buds, tracks, tools, or states, depending on the subject. Avoid generic stock photography, equal bouquets, and decorative anatomy fragments.

### Object / artifact

Use assembled silhouette, joins, function, wear, material, use traces, and meaningful instances. Prefer an editorial object family over a product still life when the carrier is a cover. Avoid catalog rows and exploded parts unless process is requested.

### Architecture / built environment

Use planes, cavities, thresholds, windows, perspective, sections, alignments, circulation, and shadow geometry. Avoid random boxes unrelated to structure.

### Human / body / portrait

Use gesture, silhouette, profile, hands, joints, fabric, gaze, movement, and shadow planes. Preserve identity and anatomy; do not scatter body parts as decoration.

### Material / abstract substance

Use grain, fold, pore, fracture, translucency, pressure, reflection, phase, density, and transformation. Give texture a host and a spatial event; avoid an empty color field with vague material adjectives.

### Diagram / data / information

Use nodes, axes, labels, evidence, sequence, comparison, and hierarchy appropriate to the information task. Do not borrow decorative HUD language from an unrelated reference.

### Narrative scene

Use a scene only when the user asks for place, time, atmosphere, or narrative. Build an authored figure-ground relation and a reading path; do not let scenery erase the carrier’s hierarchy.

## Subject grammar versus recipe

The recipe decides how pressure is distributed. Theme grammar decides what the pressure is made of. Neither should replace the other.

```text
recipe = where / how the viewer reads
subject grammar = what the viewer reads
```

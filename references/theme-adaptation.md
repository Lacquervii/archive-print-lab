# Theme adaptation protocol

A good adaptation is not a reference layout with a new noun and not a pale object with decorative lines removed.

```text
fixed family relationships
+ selected mode and recipe
+ current theme gestalt
+ current material proof
+ current palette and carriers
+ generator-ready spatial prompt
```

## Re-author every theme

Resolve the subject proposition, complete primary gestalt, related forms, medium-scale events, material proof, title fit, carrier provenance, chassis translation, palette roles, density, protected corridors, release, and refusal list.

Required palette roles:

```text
field
volume
material
accent
secondary or null
title_ink
graphic_ink
release
forbidden inherited hues
freshness rationale
```

A phrase such as “restrained accent” is not a palette record.

## Title fit

Choose contained, edge-touching, partial crop, stacked, secondary, or no title based on title length, aspect ratio, subject width, and viewer action. The default short title remains fully inside the canvas with a small top breathing gap. Use short text because most generators render lettering approximately.

## Carrier provenance

Classify each structural member as:

- theme-native: a contour, shadow, fold, branch, seam, cavity, path, or material boundary;
- carrier-native: title, index, edition, exhibition, or communication logic;
- profile-native: the family’s translucent printed planes, dark anchors, sparse marks, and registration behavior.

A profile-native member is valid when it has a page job and visible contact. It must not become copied source geometry or fake data.

## Series consistency

Compare outputs by:

1. macro → meso → subject → release skeleton;
2. title authority and subject contact;
3. compact central pressure and unequal depth;
4. visible contacting field;
5. soft/hard separation;
6. localized dark pressure;
7. fresh theme-derived palette.

The skeleton should remain recognizable while the subject, material, palette, title, and carrier vocabulary change.

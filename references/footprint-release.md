# Footprint and release control

A rich center does not need a large outer silhouette.

## Subject envelope

Define the smallest believable outer contour that contains the complete central event and its necessary echoes. Keep a continuous title corridor, at least one breathing side field, and a lower or lateral release corridor.

## Internal richness

Build inward with:

- unequal front/middle/rear forms;
- overlap and occlusion;
- localized dark anchors;
- material proof with a host;
- contained shadows, reflections, or whole-form echoes;
- a readable medium-scale transition or junction.

Do not solve a weak center by expanding it to every edge.

## Release

Reserve the release before the subject grows. It may contain a fading plane, contour, shadow, paper tooth, or tiny subject-relevant inscription. It must feel intentionally shaped, not merely leftover.

## Review

At thumbnail size, the title, bounded subject, and release should all remain legible. At normal size, meso contacts and material evidence should appear. If the image feels crowded, reduce the footprint before deleting the subject’s internal structure.

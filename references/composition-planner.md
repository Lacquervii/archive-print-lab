# Composition planner — turn a compiled theme into a page event

## Purpose

A theme compiler answers **what the image contains**. This planner answers **how the page behaves**. It prevents both content-led still lifes and generic template reskins.

```text
compiled theme + selected mode/recipe + chassis intensity
-> macro / central / meso / chassis / release scene graph
-> natural spatial prompt
```

## Plan these roles before writing prose

### 1. Macro / first attention

Choose title, glyph mass, dominant image event or material field. For the canonical Archive Print Lab cover, title authority must carry **1/3–1/2 of total page visual mass (default target: about 2/5)**, with a small clean breathing gap of approximately **3%–5% of canvas height above the title**. Keep the title fully inside the canvas with narrow but definite side margins; do not force edge-to-edge bleed or accidental cropping. Record its occupied field, contrast, contact with the compact central event and protected top gap. The title must read before the subject at thumbnail scale; if it reads like a header, enlarge it and reduce the subject’s outer envelope. A title is not automatically required only for an explicit alternate mode.

### 2. Central event

Define the primary gestalt's outer envelope, dominant relation, support hierarchy, internal density sources and edge entries. Keep external footprint separate from internal richness.

### 3. Meso relationship field

Place the one or two normal-distance events from the compiler. State their direction, scale difference and front/back behavior. This can be theme-native, carrier-native, profile-native chassis, or a deliberate combination—record the provenance instead of pretending all fields have the same source.

### 4. Graphic chassis

If `graphic-chassis.md` selected `light`, `active` or `structural`, decide the exact visible jobs:

- macro typography plane;
- translucent printed planes/outlined frames/blocks/paths;
- sparse index marks;
- print registration or scan behavior.

For a canonical structural cover, the meso field has a pressure floor: compile one primary carrier, one unequal support carrier, and one return carrier that disappears behind the subject and reappears toward the release. They may be theme-native, carrier-native or profile-native, but they must read as one unequal field at normal distance and create real front/back contacts. Do not let a backend adaptation reduce this to one faint line or one isolated circle.

### 5. Release and corridors

Reserve continuous areas before the center grows:

- title corridor;
- at least one lateral breathing field when the carrier needs it;
- lower/peripheral release;
- reading path between macro, event and release.

A release gets a low-weight continuation—paper tooth, a fading shadow, a final plane, a short label, a material residue—not a second subject or a dead blank. On canonical covers, align the tiny lowercase editorial imprint `archive-print-lab` to this release or its existing rule/axis; it is a series colophon, not a watermark.

## Contact matrix

For an active or structural relational cover, write at least three different visible contacts. They may be chosen from:

```text
macro is interrupted by central event
flat plane passes behind subject
flat plane is cut by subject and reappears
shadow/contour aligns with a plane
subject crosses a title baseline
one dark block anchors behind a light material form
release inherits a fading crop/plane/material trace
```

Do not force every relation onto a hero or quiet plate. If the selected recipe does not need a four-rung field, plan fewer but stronger relationships.

## Review before rendering

Hide the subject nouns and test the plan:

- Does macro -> central -> release still describe a page with pressure?
- If a chassis is active, does it have a real spatial job rather than a decorative count?
- If chassis is absent, did the central event gain enough theme-native pressure?
- Does the envelope protect usable corridors without shrinking the theme into an icon?
- Is the plan specific to this subject, not a literal 9010 coordinate trace?

Only then render a model-facing description.
